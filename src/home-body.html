Skin

Material Nº 1 — Skin · the player

Your hand
already knows.

Ten years of guitar technique, replaced by the one gesture you've made every day since you were two — a swipe. These strings are live. Drag across them.

That was you. Six strings, sounding in the order your hand crossed them, the instant you crossed them. No chords memorized. No calluses. No ten years.

Rosetta is three things: a guitar made of glass, a songwriter named Mo, and a box that turns real wood into your speaker. This page demonstrates all three — audibly.

Material Nº 2 — Glass · the app

A guitar made of glass.

Open the app and you're in the jam room — no menu, no course, no setup. Pick a key and a mode; Rosetta voices every chord properly, string by string, and the chords stack in front of you as bands. You supply the one thing no machine should: the rhythm.

Mind

Material Nº 3 — Mind · Mo, the songwriter

Mo writes. You play.

Describe a song — a mood, a memory, a title — and Mo writes the whole thing in the only format that matters here: one your hands can play. Chords, structure, strumming pattern, key, tempo. Never lyrics; songs travel as chord recipes, which is why they're yours to keep and share.

Straight talk Mo gets most songs right, and sometimes gets the feel wrong. Tell it what's off — it revises, and the first fix after a song is free. A song Mo fails to deliver costs you nothing at all.

Wood

Material Nº 4 — Wood · the hardware

Your guitar. As the speaker.

Inside the box is a transducer that holds against your guitar's soundboard. A guitar makes sound when its top vibrates — and the top doesn't care what set it moving. So what you hear isn't a recording of some guitar. It's your guitar's own body, singing in your own room.

Coda · the deal

The guitar is free. Forever. Jam, library, campfire, sound rack — no ads, no account needed, no trial clock.

Mo is metered. Some songs free every month. Honest numbers land at launch, once we know exactly what a song costs to write — not before.

Failure is free too. If Mo ever fails to deliver your song, it costs you nothing.

Do I need a guitar?

No. The phone is the instrument. The hardware is for when you want your own guitar to be the speaker.

Do I need to know chords?

No. The app voices them — properly, string by string. You supply the rhythm.

Is it free?

The app and jam are free forever. Mo is metered — you get some songs free every month.

Does it work on Android?

Not yet. iPhone first.

Can I use my own songs?

Yes — describe one to Mo, or import a song someone shared with you. Songs travel as self-contained files.

Why are there no lyrics?

Chords and rhythms aren't copyrightable; lyrics are. Rosetta ships zero copyrighted content, deliberately.
